## Orchestration & Choreography

Two ways to run a process across services: one service drives the steps, or each service reacts to events.

<details><summary>Show topics</summary>

- Choreography: each service listens to events and sends its own; no central service.
- Orchestration: an orchestrator sends commands to each service and waits for the replies.
- Who knows where the process is: in choreography nobody, unless one service tracks status per order; in
  orchestration the orchestrator.
- Stuck process: alert on a status held too long; the orchestrator's timeout per step.
- Costs: choreography hides the flow and can tie services in cycles; orchestration adds a central service that can
  grab all the logic.
- When to pick which: number of steps, how often the flow changes, how many teams own it.
- Where the orchestrator keeps its state; workflow engines → Durable Execution.

</details>
