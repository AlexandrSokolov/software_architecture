# Polling for changes

## Why poll

- When do you have to ask for changes instead of being told about them?
- You poll every 5 minutes. What does a shorter or a longer interval cost?

## Where the next poll starts

- What do you save between polls to know where to continue: a time, an ID, or something else?
- You poll another system by its "updated at" time. Whose clock must the next poll start from?
- You save the "last seen" point before handling the rows, or after. What does each order risk?

## Rows you can miss

- You poll rows "newer than the last one seen", by time or by ID. Which rows can you skip?
- A row changed three times between two polls. What do you see, and what is gone?
- A row was deleted. Why does your poll never see it?
- You page through the results while rows keep changing. Which rows can slip past you?

## Rows you get twice

- To avoid skipping rows, you re-read the last few minutes every time. What new problem do you get?
- The poller crashed after handling the rows but before saving its point. What happens next?
- Many rows share the last "updated at" value. Do you ask for ">" or ">="?

## Big backlogs

- Your poller was off for two days. The first poll returns a million rows. What breaks?
- The API allows 100 calls per minute. How does that shape your polling?

## Change data capture instead

- What does change data capture give you that polling can't?
- What does it cost, and when can't you use it?
