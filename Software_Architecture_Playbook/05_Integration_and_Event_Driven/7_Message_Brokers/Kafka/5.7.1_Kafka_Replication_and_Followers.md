
### How can a Kafka broker lose a message?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Inside, Kafka is [single-leader replication](#kafka-broker--architecture). Messages get lost at the same points as in
any replicated database, in this order:

- **[A follower falls behind](#kafka-follower-down--how-does-it-catch-up)** — it must catch up; old messages may
  already be deleted by retention, for followers and consumers alike.
- **[In-sync copies run out](#producer-ack--when-does-the-leader-send-it)** — the ACK can mean "on one machine
  only"; `min.insync.replicas` decides whether to refuse writes or accept that risk.
- **[The leader dies](#kafka-leader-dies--what-happens)** — failover; messages only the old leader had are lost.
- **[All copies go down at once](#kafka-power-cut-on-all-copies--whats-lost)** — confirmed messages were still only
  in memory.

The settings that close these gaps: [one checklist](#broker-crash--how-to-keep-acked-messages).

Handle: one copy behind → too few copies in sync → the leader gone → every copy gone at once.

</details>

### Kafka broker — architecture?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Kafka has two architectural levels:

- **Outside — Producers & Consumers.** Your services use Kafka. A **producer** sends a message to a topic
  ("order updated"); **consumers** pull it. To your code the broker looks like one thing.
    - **Goal:** Decouple external microservices and enable asynchronous communication.
    - **Questions:** who gets the message, duplicates, order, consumer groups.
- **Inside — the cluster (leader & followers).**
  It works like a distributed database: each partition is stored on several machines (brokers).
  One broker is the **leader** of the partition; the other brokers are **followers**: they copy from the leader.
  The client library finds the leader itself: the producer writes to it, and consumers read from it too (by default).
    - **Goal:** Data durability and High Availability of the broker itself.
    - **Questions:** a broker died — is the message safe, and can producers still write?

Where the levels meet:

- The producer writes to the leader, and consumers read from the leader too (by default).
- A consumer sees a message only after every in-sync follower (one that keeps up with the leader) has copied it,
  so it never reads a message that a leader crash could still erase.

This is single-leader replication, the same as a database with replicas: producers and consumers are its clients,
leader and followers are how it stores data. Followers fetch from the leader the same way consumers do, but they
keep a copy instead of processing the message.

</details>

### Kafka — which replication setup?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

**Kafka's choice:** no fixed sync follower. The leader waits for every follower that keeps up; a follower that falls
behind is dropped from that list, so one slow node never stops writes.

**Default (since 3.0, `acks=all`):** wait for the followers that keep up — but if none is left, the leader confirms
alone. Semi-sync that quietly turns async. Before 3.0 (`acks=1`): fully async.

**What you can change:**

- **Wait or not** — per producer: confirm on the leader alone (async) or after the followers that keep up.
- **Minimum copies** — per topic: refuse writes when too few copies keep up (2 of 3 → every ACK is on two machines,
  i.e. semi-sync, which with 3 copies is also a majority).

Handle: Kafka waits for whoever keeps up; you decide whether to wait, and the minimum it must reach.

</details>

### Consumer reads — which broker serves them?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Two ways in Kafka:

- [**The leader**](#reading-from-the-leader--how) — the default in every version.
- [**A nearby follower**](#reading-from-a-nearby-follower--how) — opt-in since Kafka 2.4, to read inside your own
  zone.

</details>

### Reading from the leader — how?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

- The consumer reads each partition from its leader — the same broker the producer writes to.
- It sees only messages that every in-sync follower has already copied.
- Config: none — this is the default. Before Kafka 2.4 it was the only way.

</details>

### Reading from a nearby follower — how?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Why: in the cloud a cluster spans several data centers (availability zones). Reading from a leader in another zone
costs network money and time.

- The consumer asks the leader; the leader names a follower in the consumer's zone; the consumer reads from it.
- Config:
    - each broker: `broker.rack=<its zone>`
    - each broker: `replica.selector.class=org.apache.kafka.common.replica.RackAwareReplicaSelector`
    - consumer: `client.rack=<its zone>`
- Cost: a follower learns which messages are safe to show a moment after the leader does, so messages reach the
  consumer slightly later.

</details>


### Kafka follower down — how does it catch up?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

A follower copies a partition the way a consumer reads it: it keeps asking the leader "give me messages from offset
N". So catch-up is the normal fetch loop with a bigger gap:

- **Find its position** — its copy of the log on disk ends at some offset. First it cuts off any messages at the end
  that the current leader doesn't have (they were never confirmed by the cluster).
- **Fetch the rest** — it asks the leader for everything after that offset.
- **Rejoin the ISR** — once it reaches the leader's end, the leader adds it back to the in-sync list
  ([ISR](#producer-ack--when-does-the-leader-send-it)).

While it is out of the ISR, `acks=all` writes don't wait for it.

Handle: find your offset, fetch the rest, rejoin the ISR.

Catch-up [can fail or get costly](#kafka-follower-catch-up--what-can-go-wrong).

</details>

### Kafka follower catch-up — what can go wrong?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

- **[Costly catch-up](#long-follower-outage--what-does-catch-up-cost)** — the leader still has the messages, but a
  large gap loads both brokers while it is sent and written.
- **[Leader's log already deleted](#leaders-log-already-deleted--now-what)** — retention removed the messages the
  follower needs; it restarts from the leader's oldest message.
- **[Follower never comes back](#dead-follower--does-the-leaders-disk-fill)** — the leader's disk does not fill,
  but the partition runs with fewer copies.

Handle: Kafka [deletes its log by time and size only](#kafka-log-retention--what-decides-deletion), never waiting for
a follower: still kept → costly gap; deleted → start from the leader's oldest; never back → fewer copies, disk fine.

</details>

### Long follower outage — what does catch-up cost?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

The follower was away long, so the gap is large. Catching it up loads both sides, on top of normal traffic:

- **Leader** — reads old log segments from disk (they are no longer in memory) and sends them over the network.
  Producers and consumers of the same broker see slower responses.
- **Follower** — writes the whole gap at full speed; its disk and network are busy. Until it is back in the ISR, the
  partition has one copy fewer.

Limit it: Kafka can cap replication speed per broker (`leader.replication.throttled.rate`,
`follower.replication.throttled.rate`) for chosen replicas — mostly used when moving partitions or adding a broker.
Cost: the follower stays out of the ISR longer.

</details>

### Kafka log retention — what decides deletion?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Each partition's log is a chain of segment files: when one is full (`segment.bytes`, 1 GB) or old (`segment.ms`,
7 days), a new one starts. Only whole closed segments are deleted. Which ones go is set per topic:

- **`cleanup.policy=delete`** (default) — by age `retention.ms` (default 7 days) or size per partition
  `retention.bytes` (default no limit). Read or not.
- **`cleanup.policy=compact`** — keeps the last message for each key; older messages with the same key are removed.

What never counts: followers and consumers. Nobody confirms anything, so a dead reader never fills the disk, but a
reader away longer than the limit [loses messages](#kafka-consumer-down-longer-than-messages-are-kept).

**Tiered storage** (`remote.storage.enable=true`) moves closed segments to object storage (e.g. S3) and keeps only
recent ones on the broker's disk, so the log can be kept much longer without big disks.

</details>

### Leader's log already deleted — now what?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

The follower asks for messages from offset N, but the leader's oldest kept message is already past N: retention
deleted the rest.

Kafka: the follower deletes its whole log for that partition and starts fetching from the leader's oldest offset. No
separate snapshot step is needed — in Kafka the log is the data, and what the leader deleted by retention is gone on
every copy anyway.

Cost: it copies the whole retained partition, like a brand-new replica.

</details>

### Dead follower — does the leader's disk fill?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

No. The leader [deletes by time and size](#kafka-log-retention--what-decides-deletion), not when followers confirm,
so a follower that never returns doesn't hold the log back.

What happens instead: the partition runs with fewer copies. The ISR is smaller, and if it drops below
`min.insync.replicas`, [`acks=all` writes are refused](#too-few-in-sync-copies--wait-or-go-on).

Fix:

- alert on broker metrics `UnderReplicatedPartitions` and `UnderMinIsrPartitionCount`;
- replace the broker: a new one with the same `broker.id` copies its partitions from the leaders, or move the
  partitions to other brokers (`kafka-reassign-partitions.sh`).

</details>

### Consumer offline — gets messages later?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Yes, by default. Kafka keeps messages in the log no matter who is connected, and a consumer group continues from its
saved position (committed offset). Three limits:

- **[Messages deleted meanwhile](#kafka-consumer-down-longer-than-messages-are-kept)** — the consumer was away longer
  than retention.
- **[Saved position deleted](#kafka-group-with-no-saved-position--where-does-it-start)** — the group had no running
  consumers longer than `offsets.retention.minutes`.
- **[New group](#kafka-group-with-no-saved-position--where-does-it-start)** — it has no position yet; by default it
  starts at the end and skips everything already there.

Cost: unread messages take disk until retention removes them.

</details>

### Kafka consumer down longer than messages are kept?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Kafka deletes old messages by time or size (`retention.ms`, `retention.bytes`), whether anyone read them or not. A
consumer that was down longer comes back with a saved position that points at deleted messages.
[`auto.offset.reset`](#kafka-group-with-no-saved-position--where-does-it-start) decides where it continues:

- `earliest` → the oldest message still kept. The deleted ones are skipped.
- `latest` → the end. Everything still kept is skipped too.
- `none` → an error; the consumer stops.

Both skips are silent. **Fix:** keep messages longer than the longest outage you plan for, and alert on consumer lag
well before it reaches that limit.

</details>

### Kafka group with no saved position — where does it start?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

A consumer group has no saved position when it is new, or when the broker deleted it. The broker deletes a group's
positions once the group has had no running consumers for `offsets.retention.minutes` (default 7 days).

Then `auto.offset.reset` decides. Its default is `latest`: start at the end. Every message already in the topic is
skipped, with no error.

**Fix:**

- set `earliest` if the group must see messages that are already there;
- set `offsets.retention.minutes` longer than the group's longest planned downtime.

</details>

### Producer ACK — when does the leader send it?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

The producer's `acks` setting:

- **`acks=0`** — no ACK; the producer doesn't wait. A failure loses the message and nobody knows.
- **`acks=1`** — ACK once the message is in the leader's own log; followers copy it a moment later. Like async
  replication.
- **`acks=all`** (`-1`) — ACK only after every in-sync replica has it. Default since Kafka 3.0; older clients
  default to `acks=1`.

**In-sync replicas (ISR)** — the leader plus the followers that reached its end within `replica.lag.time.max.ms`
(default 30 s). Kafka has no sync or async followers: all fetch the same way, and `acks` decides whether the leader
waits. A follower that falls behind is dropped from the ISR, and the leader stops waiting for it; it rejoins once it
catches up. The cost that stays: each `acks=all` send waits for the slowest follower still in the ISR.

Consumers see a message only after every ISR member has it (the **high watermark**), whatever `acks` the producer
used.

</details>

### In-sync follower dies — do writes hang?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Only for a few seconds. Kafka doesn't wait for a fixed follower; it drops the dead one from the ISR and goes on:

- **Follower stops fetching** — `acks=all` writes wait for it.
- **It is dropped from the ISR** — after `replica.lag.time.max.ms` (30 s), or sooner when the controller sees the
  whole broker is gone (`broker.session.timeout.ms`, 9 s).
- **Writes are confirmed again** — now by the smaller ISR.

Cost: writes stall for those seconds, and the ISR can
[shrink to the leader alone](#kafka-leader-waits-for-all-copies--how-is-it-lost).

</details>

### Kafka leader waits for all copies — how is it lost?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

`acks=all` waits for all **in-sync** copies, not all copies. A follower that falls behind (slow disk, long GC pause,
network trouble) is dropped from the in-sync list. The list can shrink to the leader alone. Then the ACK comes when one
machine has the message. If that machine's disk is lost, or an
[out-of-date copy takes over](#kafka-out-of-date-copy-becomes-leader--whats-lost), the message is gone.

**Fix:** [a floor on the in-sync copies](#too-few-in-sync-copies--wait-or-go-on) — `min.insync.replicas=2`.

</details>

### Too few in-sync copies — wait or go on?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

`min.insync.replicas` (topic or broker) is the floor for `acks=all` writes; `acks=1` writes ignore it.

- **Refuse writes** — `min.insync.replicas=2` with `replication.factor=3`. When fewer than 2 replicas (leader
  included) are in sync, the leader rejects the write (`NotEnoughReplicas`); the producer retries, then gets an error.
    - Pro: every ACKed message is on at least 2 brokers; one broker can be down while writes still go through.
    - Con: writes fail until a follower catches up.
- **Keep accepting** — `min.insync.replicas=1` (default). An ISR of the leader alone still confirms `acks=all`
  writes, so `acks=all` falls back to `acks=1`.
    - Pro: writes stay up.
    - Con: the ACK means one machine; if it dies, the message is gone.

3 copies with a floor of 2 is close to semi-synchronous replication: each ACK means the leader and at least one
follower have the message.

Handle:

- refuse when losing a confirmed message is worse than failed writes (orders, payments);
- accept when staying up matters more (metrics, logs).

</details>
