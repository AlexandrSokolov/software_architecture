### Kafka leader dies — what happens?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

**Failover**, always automatic, run by the controller. A three-step chain:

- **[Detect](#kafka-leader-dead--who-notices)** — the controller decides the broker is dead.
- **Choose** — [the controller decides](#new-kafka-leader--who-decides) and
  [picks a follower from the ISR](#new-kafka-leader--which-follower).
- **[Reconfigure](#after-kafka-failover--who-must-switch)** — clients, other followers, and the old leader follow
  the new leader.

It [has risks](#kafka-failover--what-can-go-wrong). A [planned change](#planned-kafka-leader-change--how) skips
detection.

Handle: controller notices, picks from the ISR, everyone follows.

</details>

### Kafka leader dead — who notices?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

The controller. Each broker sends it heartbeats; no heartbeat for `broker.session.timeout.ms` (default 9 s) → the
controller fences the broker: removes it from every ISR and moves leadership of its partitions. (Old ZooKeeper mode:
the broker's ZooKeeper session expires, `zookeeper.session.timeout.ms`, 18 s.)

As with any timeout, a dead, slow, or cut-off broker looks the same; how long to wait is
[a trade-off](#kafka-failover-timeout--how-long).

</details>

### New Kafka leader — who decides?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

The **active controller** — one node appoints leaders for every partition (the controller-node approach, not a vote
per partition). All brokers and clients learn the new leader from the controller's metadata.

Who the active controller is needs agreement: in KRaft mode (only mode since Kafka 4.0) 3 or 5 controller nodes pick
it with Raft. In old ZooKeeper mode, ZooKeeper picked one broker as controller.

</details>

### New Kafka leader — which follower?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

The first replica in the partition's assignment list that is alive and in the ISR.

No need to search for the most up-to-date one: every ISR member has all messages up to the high watermark, i.e.
everything confirmed with `acks=all`. Messages only the old leader had (confirmed with
[`acks=1`](#kafka-leader-acked-then-died--why-lost)) are lost.

No ISR member alive → [wait, or let an out-of-date copy lead](#kafka-out-of-date-copy-becomes-leader--whats-lost).

</details>

### After Kafka failover — who must switch?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

- **Producers and consumers** — their next request to the old leader fails or returns `NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER`. The
  client library refreshes metadata, finds the new leader, and the producer resends (it keeps retrying until
  `delivery.timeout.ms`, default 2 min). No DNS or IP to move.
- **Other followers** — fetch from the new leader; they cut off messages it doesn't have.
- **Old leader** — when it comes back, the controller tells it it's a follower. It cuts its log back to where the new
  leader's term started (found by **leader epoch**), then
  [catches up](#kafka-follower-down--how-does-it-catch-up).

</details>

### Planned Kafka leader change — how?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

- **Before stopping a broker — controlled shutdown** (`controlled.shutdown.enable=true`, default). The broker asks
  the controller to move leadership of its partitions to other ISR members, then stops. No detection timeout, nothing
  lost; clients retry for a moment.
- **After it comes back — preferred leader election.** Leadership returns to the first replica in each partition's
  list, so it doesn't stay piled on the brokers that stayed up. Automatic (`auto.leader.rebalance.enable=true`,
  checked every 5 min) or by hand (`kafka-leader-election.sh --election-type preferred`).

Handle: move leaders off, stop, restart, move them back. Field term: **controlled shutdown**.

</details>

### Kafka failover — what can go wrong?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

- **[Lost writes](#kafka-leader-acked-then-died--why-lost)** — with `acks=1`, the old leader's last messages never
  reached a follower.
- **[Out-of-date copy leads](#kafka-out-of-date-copy-becomes-leader--whats-lost)** — no in-sync copy is left; letting
  a lagging one lead loses even `acks=all` messages.
- **[Two leaders](#kafka-leader-cut-off-from-the-cluster--whats-lost)** — the old leader is cut off but still takes
  writes.
- **[Wrong timeout](#kafka-failover-timeout--how-long)** —
    - too long → partitions without a leader longer;
    - too short → leaders move when the broker was fine.

Kafka [has no manual failover mode](#kafka-failover--automatic-or-manual); the human choice is whether to accept
loss.

Handle: confirmed by the leader only → lost; nobody in sync → lose or wait; cut-off leader → `acks` decides; timeout
→ false alarm vs downtime.

</details>

### Kafka leader acked, then died — why lost?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

**`acks=1`** — the ACK goes out once the message is in the leader's own log; followers copy it a moment later. If
the leader dies in between, each step causes the next:

- **New leader lacks it** — a follower that never got the message becomes the new leader.
- **Old leader drops it** — it comes back as a follower and cuts its log to match the new leader.
- **Nobody notices** — the producer already got the ACK, so it never resends. No error anywhere.
- **Offsets are reused** — the new leader gives its next messages the same offsets. A producer that kept the offset
  from its ACK now points at a different message. Consumers are safe: they never saw the lost one, because they read
  only up to the high watermark.

**`acks=all`** closes this, with two gaps left:

- [the ISR shrank to the leader alone](#kafka-leader-waits-for-all-copies--how-is-it-lost) — same as `acks=1`;
- [no in-sync follower is alive, and an out-of-sync one leads](#kafka-out-of-date-copy-becomes-leader--whats-lost).

</details>

### Kafka out-of-date copy becomes leader — what's lost?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

The leader dies, and every copy left had fallen behind (none is in sync). If one of them is allowed to lead
(`unclean.leader.election.enable=true`), every message the old leader had and this copy didn't is gone, even ones
confirmed with `acks=all`. When the old leader returns, it cuts those messages from its log to match.

The other choice is to wait for an in-sync copy to come back. Nothing is lost, but the partition takes no reads or
writes until then. Kafka waits by default (`false`): it picks not losing data over staying up.

</details>

### Kafka leader cut off from the cluster — what's lost?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

The leader loses contact with the controller and the other brokers, but some producers can still reach it. The
controller picks a new leader from the in-sync copies. The old leader doesn't know yet and keeps taking writes, so for
a while there are two leaders (**split brain**):

- **`acks=1`:** the old leader confirms each write with only its own copy. When it reconnects, it learns about the new
  leader and cuts those messages from its log → confirmed messages are lost.
- **`acks=all`:** the followers now copy from the new leader, and the old leader can't shrink the in-sync list on its
  own; that change needs the controller. No write gets all its copies, so the producer gets a timeout, retries, and
  finds the new leader. Nothing confirmed is lost.

What stops the old leader: each new leader gets a higher **leader epoch** (a fencing token). Followers fetch with the
new epoch, so they ignore the old leader; when it sees the higher epoch, it steps down.

</details>

### Kafka failover timeout — how long?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Two timeouts, same trade-off:

- **`broker.session.timeout.ms`** (9 s) — when the controller treats a broker as dead and moves its leaders.
    - Too long → a dead broker's partitions have no leader that long: no writes, no reads.
    - Too short → a GC pause or network glitch fences a healthy broker; leaders move, clients retry, and it may flap
      back and forth.
- **`replica.lag.time.max.ms`** (30 s) — when a follower is dropped from the ISR.
    - Too long → `acks=all` writes wait that long for a dead follower.
    - Too short → followers drop out on every short spike; the ISR keeps shrinking, and with
      `min.insync.replicas=2` writes start failing.

Handle: false alarm vs longer downtime.

</details>

### Kafka failover — automatic or manual?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Always automatic among in-sync copies. That choice is safe — any ISR member has every `acks=all` message — so Kafka
needs no human for it.

The human decision is the case with no in-sync copy left:

- **Wait** (default) — the partition is down until an in-sync broker returns.
- **Accept loss** — an admin lets an out-of-date copy lead: per topic `unclean.leader.election.enable=true`, or once
  for one partition with `kafka-leader-election.sh --election-type unclean`.

Accept loss when the partition being down costs more than its last messages (metrics); wait otherwise.

</details>

### Kafka power cut on all copies — what's lost?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

Kafka confirms a message once it is in the operating system's memory (the page cache). The OS writes it to disk a bit
later. Kafka doesn't force each write to disk; it counts on copies on other machines instead.

If all copies lose power at the same moment (same rack, same power line), every message not yet on disk is lost on all
of them, even ones confirmed with `acks=all`.

**Fix:** put the copies in different racks or zones (`broker.rack`), so one power cut can't hit them all. Forcing every
write to disk (`flush.messages=1`) also works, but makes writes much slower.

</details>

### Broker crash — how to keep acked messages?
<details><summary>Show answer</summary>

- **`replication.factor=3`** — three copies of each partition.
- **[`acks=all`](#producer-ack--when-does-the-leader-send-it)** (producer) — ACK only after every in-sync replica
  has the message.
- **[`min.insync.replicas=2`](#too-few-in-sync-copies--wait-or-go-on)** — the ACK means at least 2 brokers; default
  1 lets `acks=all` fall back to `acks=1`.
- **[`unclean.leader.election.enable=false`](#kafka-out-of-date-copy-becomes-leader--whats-lost)** (default) — only
  an in-sync follower can become leader.
- **[`broker.rack`](#kafka-power-cut-on-all-copies--whats-lost)** — copies in different racks or zones.
- **Consumers** — [retention](#kafka-consumer-down-longer-than-messages-are-kept) and
  [`offsets.retention.minutes`](#kafka-group-with-no-saved-position--where-does-it-start) longer than the longest
  planned outage; alert on lag.

Handle: enough copies, wait for them, never let a lagging one lead, never in one rack — and keep messages longer than
anyone is away.

</details>
